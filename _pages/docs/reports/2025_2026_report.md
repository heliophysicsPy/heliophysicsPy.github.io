Due to changes in NASA's reporting requirements, reports from this point forward will be annual reports (with this particular report serving as a 2-year report to cover the 2025 and 2026 period).

**<u>Major Goals and Objectives</u>**

LASP will continue to coordinate the PyHC. We have held ten plus community meetings, consisting of a mix of in-person, hybrid, and virtual meetings. These have resulted in a mission statement, a set of written standards for Python software projects, the formation of new working groups to address specific topics, a PyHC-related workshop to discuss the future of the community and its technical priorities, the 2022 and 2024 PyHC summer schools, and a series of meeting reports, all of which are available on the pyhc.org website. We will continue to organize meetings on a bi-annual (twice-yearly) basis, one fully virtual and one hybrid.

The PyHC leadership team consists of the PyHC Lead and the PyHC Technical Lead. The PyHC Lead is the main point of contact, and initiator/lead orchestrator, of all PyHC-sponsored activities. They are also the main source of PyHC outreach, travelling to local and international conferences to grow PyHC. The PyHC Technical Lead supports Python projects in the community by coordinating technical efforts, advising developers on code standards and contribution to community repositories, aiding the PyHC Lead, and coordinating educational efforts. Both individuals will be employed at LASP.

The PyHC Lead will:

- hold telecons and meetings to:

  - improve support and visibility for existing and new Python libraries used by multiple projects;

  - identify other projects which could benefit from either conversion to Python, or development of APIs to allow use by Python;

  - promote best practices for documentation and testing, particularly of code submitted by the community;

  - organize and workshop community-generated ideas for package improvement, interoperability, and integration (e.g., hack use case examples for the PyHC Gallery).

- organize and hold the regular (approximately bi-weekly) telecons;

- coordinate the spring 2025 and spring 2026 fully virtual meetings, and the fall 2025 and fall 2026 hybrid meetings in Boulder and online (to allow participants unable to travel to participate);

- work with the PyHC Technical Lead other leaders in the PyHC to plan the PyHC 2026 Summer School (see Summer School section for more details);

- attend AGU 2025 and 2026 to coordinate a Python session and publicize PyHC's efforts. The PyHC Lead will solicit presentations from the software teams involved in this project and encourage interested outside entities to submit them;

- attend several relevant conferences, workshops, and meetings to publicize PyHC's efforts, improve communication with those outside of PyHC, and foster current and potential future collaborations with PyHC;

- work with PyHC package leads to determine who will attend science-focused meetings (SHINE, CEDAR, GEM) each year, as well as other related meetings (e.g., US-RSE annual meetings and SciPy meetings). Selections will be made based on need, relevance, and that year's meeting themes;

- work with the PyHC membership to continue improving our outreach--outside of travel--to the scientific community (e.g., through social media, increased PyHC Blog use, improved Gallery examples, putting PyHC updates in a newsletter, and virtual office hours);

- assist the PyHC Technical Lead in maintaining and improving the pyhc.org website; and

- continue to maintain regular contact with NASA HDRL, consisting of quarterly status reports, and one-off meetings as necessary. The pyhc.org website will be updated to contain any recommendations and standards developed by the group, documenting all meetings and providing links to member projects and useful resources.

The PyHC Technical Lead will:

- serve as science and software technical point of contact for PyHC and community building;

- develop and maintain the list of compliance criteria for code inclusion or association with PyHC;

- work with PyHC community developers to evaluate code submitted to PyHC for standards compliance;

- help new community members learn how to contribute code;

- maintain and update the PyHC Gallery page;

- help to establish the registration of PyHC software and to assign DOIs to code, as well as create a page for DOIs on the PyHC website;

- maintain the PyHC website, as well as improve upon the organization, discoverability, and understanding of projects (e.g., working with community developers to create a table of package routines/descriptions and areas of "help wanted" in code), populate the Blog section with project updates, intros, new features, and other relevant information, and make it easier to find information such as the standards document, meeting details, and project tutorials (video and gallery examples);

- assist in coordinating and running a PyHC 2026 Summer School;

- co-lead and organize efforts around the Science Platforms Coordination IHDEA working group alongside key members in the Heliophysics and space physics disciplines;

- develop and expand the reach of PyHC-Chat (a ChatGPT4-powered AI bot that will aid in the discovery, findability, and understanding of how to leverage PyHC packages; and

- maintain and improve upon the PyHC common software environment.

***PyHC 2027 Winter School***

Due to funding timelines for participant travel support, the PyHC Leadership team made the choice to move the originally planned PyHC 2026 Summer School to January of 2027, as a winter school. The PyHC 2027 winter school will build off the success of the 2022 and 2024 summer schools. The PyHC Summer School series presents a unique opportunity to connect with the broader Heliophysics and space weather community and communicate the benefits of leveraging PyHC's open-source Python ecosystem. In particular, the ability to get the up-and-coming generation involved in PyHC activities and working with PyHC packages ensures PyHC's utility, sustainability, and longevity. The PyHC 2027 Winter School will include PyHC core project (and other relevant PyHC packages) introductions and demonstrations, followed by example science use cases that meeting attendees can hack together using PyHC packages. We will continue with the PyHC challenge ladder that was successful in the PyHC 2024 summer school.  Lastly, we will also continue to partner with HelioCloud (<https://heliocloud.org/>), as well include a day to present several HDRL resources to winter school participants.

The result of the Summer School is increasing the users--and potential contributors--of PyHC packages, thus promoting PyHC package sustainability and maintenance, as well as PyHC outreach efforts. The PyHC Lead will coordinate, organize, plan, and work with other key PyHC leadership to create and implement the PyHC 2027 Winter School. The PyHC Technical Lead will assist as needed in preparing technical materials (e.g., a web page for the summer school, helping organize technical topics, updating the PyHC Challenge Ladder exercises, or getting Python environments set up for meeting attendees). Like the PyHC 2022 and 2024 summer schools, we intend to have all packages upload all demos and tutorials to a HelioCloud instance. This ensures that students can avoid the dependency issues of installing PyHC software locally, providing summer school attendees with a robust, standardized platform which works "out of the box", and allow them to focus on learning instead of debugging.

3\) Significant Results/Key Outcomes

4\) Other Achievements

- Successfully passed PHEP 3 (second vote), PHEP 4 (second vote) and PHEP 5 (first vote), furthering our PyHC's goals to set and maintain community-appropriate standards.

- Upgraded the PyHC environment to Python 3.12 (per PHEP 3) and tested a fully SPEC 0-compliant version (and made GitHub issues to address issues, e.g., updates such as switching to uv from pip)

- Worked with the SpacePy package lead, Jon Niehof, to fix the Kamodo package pip install issues (a longstanding issue)

- Updated the PyHC stand-alone standards document and minted a new DOI ([link](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.2529130))

- Continued working with the HSSI team to incorporate PyHC developer feedback on the resource submission form and overall site. Further enhanced PyHC package discoverability through creating newly designed landing pages with embedded JSON-LD for discoverability and connections with other important Heliophysics resources in publications (SciX) and data (HelioData), as well as submitting new PyHC packages to the HSSI and enhanced metadata for existing submissions (through manual submissions as well with the new HSSI Metadata Orchestrator AI tool).
